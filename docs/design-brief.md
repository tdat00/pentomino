# Pentomino Puzzle Studio — Design brief

> **Living document:** kept in sync with the current code. The original design is in `docs/init-design/`; the developer specification is `design-detail.md`.

## Purpose and main journey

An account-free puzzle workshop for players, enthusiasts, parents, and teachers. Create challenges in the **Generator** and solve them in the **Player**.

**Home → Generator: Create board → Create piece set (New piece set) → Create puzzles (New puzzle) → Play → Player (new tab)**

Visiting `tdat00.github.io/pentomino` opens the Generator. The Player also opens shared and bookmarked links without using the Generator.

Generated pieces contain at least three joined squares. Sizes vary, but shapes never repeat after turning or flipping. Colors, patterns, and one-character labels (past Z the letter is drawn in a circle, a square or a black disc) help distinguish them; colors and letters are drawn so they stay apart on a black-and-white print.

## Generator: make a challenge

The Generator is a three-step wizard. The steps sit on the right, each in a frame with its own settings and button; the main panel shows the current step, with **‹ Step N - name** and **Step N - name ›** at its top right; the puzzles made so far are listed on the left. The step is part of the address (`?step=2`), so reloads, Back/Forward and shared links return to it. Language (EN | VI) and theme (light, dark, high contrast; the system's light or dark by default) are switched in the header; on a phone (below 768 px) the header keeps only icons, and a **Settings** button opens a dialog with the two. On a phone the three steps are three frames of one column as on a wide screen (the header, and the summary of a finished step under it), the screen of the current step under its frame, and the settings and button of the current step a bar fixed to the bottom of the screen; the puzzles are shown one at a time with ◀ ▶ buttons, like the piece sets, instead of cards.

1. **Create board.** Choose a preset (Rectangle, Heart, Diamond, Cross, Ring) and enter rows and columns. Click or drag cells to block/unblock them; keep the playable area connected. Zoom, fit, a move tool, undo (Ctrl+Z), a square counter and warnings help.

2. **Turn the view.** Rotate in 45° steps with ⟲ ⟳ in the board's toolbar. **This never changes the grid, blocked cells, pieces, or difficulty.** The Heart preset is a square with its top-right quarter blocked, displayed at 45° counter-clockwise. Paint directly in the turned view.

3. **Create piece set.** A table compares every level on this board: piece count, piece sizes and pre-placed pieces, each with a "?" that explains the number. Levels the board cannot use are greyed out with the reason. The step frame holds a repeatable text seed (type one, or roll one with 🎲; leave it empty for a new random seed each time) and **New piece set**. The app verifies the pieces fill the board and finds a matching sample puzzle before showing the set.

4. **Browse piece sets.** The set's pieces and a summary ("Hard | 13 pieces | Piece sizes 7–12 | Seed …") appear under the table. ◀ Set n of total ▶ steps through the sets made for this board (up to 50); Remove and Remove all clean up; **Select this piece set** under the pieces moves on to step 3 with it. A set that repeats one in the list is not added again—the existing one is shown.

5. **Create puzzles.** Choose the puzzle level (the set's level by default) and, optionally, **Require unique solution**—experimental for Hell puzzles above 200 squares, where it may find no result. Each **New puzzle** press adds one puzzle with its own random seed and a genuinely different arrangement; a puzzle equal to one already listed is never added. Some pieces are locked; at least two remain to play.

6. **Inspect and launch.** Cards on the left, newest first, show "Puzzle #n", the estimated level (bold) and score, the seed, the pieces to place and a preview. The solution status updates separately: Checking…, Unique solution ✓, Multiple solutions, At least 1 solution, No solution exists, or Uniqueness not checked. Clicking a card shows that puzzle in the main panel (otherwise the newest one) with its board and pieces to place; **Play** opens it in the Player in a new tab and need not wait, **Print** (right of Play) prepares it for paper (see "Print on paper" below), and **Show solution** (under the board, at its right) draws its solution on the board until pressed again. **Import puzzle** (the header button, left of the language switch; only its icon on a phone) re-creates a puzzle from a link copied from the Player: its board, piece set and puzzle appear as a new, *Imported* set (a puzzle of the set on show joins that set; a puzzle already listed is not added twice; New puzzle then works on the imported set, whose solution comes from the link or is found in the background), and a link that cannot be read is explained in the dialog without changing anything.

Board edits never delete work: the lists switch to the piece sets and puzzles of the new board (or empty lists), and going back to an earlier board brings its sets back. View changes preserve everything. A different difficulty may need a new piece set. The board, the settings and the sets of the last 10 boards are kept in this browser; none of it is in the address, which holds only the step. Puzzles travel through Play instead (a `#p=` link also opens Import puzzle).

## Levels and typical boards

Very Easy/Easy gives many starting pieces; Normal/Hard offers more choices; Expert/Master gives less guidance; Nightmare/Hell starts with very few pieces. One shared scoring method supplies **estimates, not promised solving times**.

| Board | Playable squares | Available levels | Example total piece counts |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 6×10 rectangle | 60 | Very Easy–Master | Easy 7–8; Master 12–13 |
| 10×10 rectangle | 100 | Easy–Nightmare | Easy 8–9; Nightmare 17–20 |
| Default Heart: 10×10 grid | 75 | Very Easy–Master | Easy 7–9; Master 13–15 |
| 18×20 rectangle | 360 | Hard–Hell-2 | Hard 16–18; Hell-1 37–42; Hell-2 50–56 |
| 22×22 rectangle | 484 | Hard–Hell-3 | Hard 17–19; Hell-3 73–80 |

Counts vary with the selected level and generated set. Blocking cells changes availability; turning the view does not. Device limits still apply.

## Player: solve comfortably

The Player looks like the Generator: a header with the title, the **EN | VI** language switch, the theme buttons (light, dark, high contrast) and **Help**, and three panels. The **puzzles** of the open pack are on the left as cards (name, rating with the level in bold, pieces to place, uniqueness, progress and a preview); the **game** is in the middle (puzzle name and rating, with Share and Print at the right of the name; the timer and move count, Undo, Redo, Hint, Replay, the hint message and the board); the **pieces to place** are on the right, under the toolbar for the selected piece. Below 1360 px the puzzles move under the game. On a phone (below 768 px) the header keeps only the icons of **Settings** (language and theme in a dialog) and **Help**, the buttons and fields are smaller (36 px), and the **pieces to place** are a bar fixed to the bottom of the screen: one row that scrolls sideways, with no turn and flip buttons; a long press on a piece opens its menu, and a swipe up lifts it onto the board. The puzzles of the pack are shown one at a time with ◀ ▶ buttons (and Remove, Remove all) above the puzzle's name, instead of cards.

Uniqueness uses the Generator's words. A puzzle that carries no uniqueness result reads **At least 1 solution**, as a new puzzle does in the Generator, once a solution is known.

> [!WARNING]
> **To be confirmed by the owner.** "Uniqueness not checked" was replaced by "At least 1 solution" to match the Generator. The Player shows it only when a solution is known: the pack includes a checked answer, or the background solvability check found one. For a puzzle shared without its answer, while that check runs or when it is inconclusive or finds no solution, the uniqueness text stays empty, so that no solvability claim appears without proof (acceptance check 11 of `design-detail.md` §13.3). Puzzles from the Generator and the sample always include their answer, so they always show it. If "At least 1 solution" should show in every case, this rule and that acceptance check must change together.

Puzzles open from their link: the Generator's Play, or an address someone shared or bookmarked. There is no file import or export. The address always holds the open puzzle, so a bookmark keeps it and sending the address shares it; a pack that did not arrive by a link (the sample, or a pack after a puzzle was removed) gets a `#p=` address in the address as soon as its codes are made. A one-puzzle address, `…/player/#p=<puzzle code>`, opens exactly that one puzzle from its code alone (see Limits, waiting, and sharing); it is never rewritten, so reloading it reopens the same puzzle. An address with several codes separated by `;`, `…/player/#p=<code>;<code>`, opens them as one pack (a list, optionally `&i=<n>` for the puzzle to open); it follows the open puzzle with `&i=`. Without a puzzle, a start screen offers the sample and a link to the Generator. A link that cannot be opened explains the problem without replacing the game.

**Entering a puzzle code.** The Player has no button for it (neither on the start screen nor in the header): the entry dialog opens by itself when a `#p=` address cannot be opened, with one labelled multi-line field. The code can be typed or pasted as it is written: the core alone, or the core with the optional part after a dot; in groups of four or not, in any case; with line breaks, spaces or hyphens; with O read as 0 and I or L as 1; or as a whole `…/player/#p=…` link, which is never fetched and follows the same rules as the address (a puzzle number other than 0, or `p` together with `pack` or `lib`, is refused). Under the field a count of the characters of the code (not of a link's prefix) shows while typing, and nothing is called an error until **Open puzzle** is pressed. On success the dialog closes and the puzzle opens; the address becomes `#p=<code>`. A code that cannot be opened keeps the text and the open game exactly as they were and says why, in English or Vietnamese: a character a code never uses, a second dot, too long, a typing error, a code needing a newer Player, a browser without compressed codes or module workers, a structure that is not a valid puzzle, or a puzzle number in a link. When exactly one wrong symbol would explain a typing error, the dialog describes it ("in group 2, character 2 may be 9 instead of 0, compare with the printout") and offers **Use this correction**; nothing changes and nothing opens until that button is pressed, and the corrected code is checked again like any other. When only the optional solution part is damaged, the dialog says so and offers **Open without the solution part**, which opens the intact core and puts only the core in the address; the damaged part is never dropped silently. A puzzle that already has saved moves comes back with them, however its code reaches the Player (typed or pasted, or read from a `#p=` address when the page opens, after a reload or when the address changes), without any notice; Replay returns to the printed clues. Cancel and Escape close the dialog and stop a code that is still being read, which can then change nothing. A `#p=` address that cannot be opened (when the page opens, or later) opens the same dialog with the address as the text and the reason.

- Click a puzzle card to play that puzzle; ✕ on a card removes it and **Remove all** empties the list. The timer starts with your first move.
- Board, tray, and dragged pieces use matching angles; labels stay upright. Every piece shows its letter where the squares are big enough, and sets of more than 20 pieces add fill patterns. Zoom and pan help on small screens.
- Large boards can use pieces of about 50 squares; many pieces lengthen the pieces panel, which scrolls with the page.
- Drag a piece, or select it, tap a board cell to preview, then confirm. A highlighted square anchors placement. Invalid drops leave the previous position unchanged.
- **Right-click a piece** (in the pieces panel or on the board), or **press and hold** it on a touch screen, for a menu: rotate clockwise, rotate counter-clockwise, flip left-right, flip top-bottom, and return to the tray. **Right-click anywhere on the board** for **Return all pieces to the tray** and **Replay the puzzle**.
- Mouse, keyboard, and touch support every move. Arrows position; E/Q turn; F/V flip; Enter places. The toolbar of the pieces panel also turns and flips the selected piece. Pieces turn in 90° steps even on a diagonal board.
- Starting pieces stay locked. Move or remove your own pieces, undo/redo, or start again with Replay.
- **Any valid completion wins:** every piece used, every playable square covered, no overlaps. Continue with Next puzzle or Replay.
- Progress saves locally when storage is available; storage failure does not prevent play. Replay erases the saved progress of the puzzle. Only the language and the theme are remembered as preferences.

## Print on paper

Both apps have a **Print** button (a printer icon: the Generator's is right of **Play**, the Player's at the right of the puzzle name) to play offline with cut-out pieces. Its dialog offers:

- **Include piece set**: every piece of the set, also those already placed on the board, in the style of an unlocked piece, ready to cut out.
- **Include board**: the playable squares. A board that is too big for a sheet is split into parts to cut out and join; a numbered mark on each shared edge shows which part goes there.
- **Print on both sides**: the back of every piece or board part is its mirror image, so when cut out both sides match (choose two-sided printing, flipped on the long edge, in the printer settings).
- **Print solution** (Generator only, off by default): the solved board after the puzzle (with two sides, on the back of the puzzle sheet; with several puzzles to a sheet, one sheet of their solutions, each behind its puzzle).
- **Board size**, a table of each size, its cell and the A4 sheets it needs, picked by a radio button in the row: Small (10 mm), Medium (15 mm), Large (20 mm) or Fit to page (the largest cell that puts the whole board on one sheet). The pieces use the same cell size, so a piece sits on the board without shifting.

**Several puzzles.** When the list of the app has more than one puzzle (the Generator's puzzles of the set, the Player's puzzles of the pack), the dialog has two columns: the options on the left and, on the right, **Puzzles per page** (1, 2 or 4) above **Puzzles to print**, a list of the puzzles in the order of the panel next to the page (newest first), each shown as "Puzzle #n · Level: score n", with a count, **Select all** and **Select none**. The list fills the height of the column and scrolls. The puzzle on show is chosen when the dialog opens. The chosen puzzles are printed in numeric order, each with its own QR code, 1, 2 (one above the other) or 4 (two by two) to a sheet, with dashed guides between them; a QR box too big for a quarter of a sheet is drawn smaller. The piece set and the board are printed once for the whole printout. With one puzzle in the list the dialog has one column and none of this.

The first sheet shows the QR code at the top left, then the puzzle's name with its level and score, then the puzzle as a small preview with the pieces to place; the preview is for reading, not for cutting. Neither the puzzle's seed nor its set's is printed, because a seed alone does not rebuild a puzzle. Print on A4 at 100 % (actual size); a 50 mm bar on the sheet lets you check it. Paper cannot turn by 45°, so a diagonal view (the Heart) is printed in its grid; the board may be turned a quarter when that saves paper.

**The printed name is the name in the list.** The sheet names a puzzle as its card does: "Puzzle #n" (English) or "Đề #n" (Vietnamese), n being its place in its list; a Player puzzle with a title of its own that is not a generated one ("Hard #3") keeps it, with " #n" added when it does not already end in "#" and digits. The code carries the puzzle's displayed heading ("Hard #3"), which is what the Player shows after a scan.

**The printed QR code rebuilds the puzzle.** The first sheet has the QR code at the top left, captioned "Play online" ("Chơi trực tuyến"), with the puzzle's name and its level and score to its right. It carries the puzzle code described under Limits, waiting, and sharing as a `#p=` link of the Player: scan it with a phone to play the same puzzle again, with no seed. No code text, address, set line or seed is printed. When the puzzle has a stored answer, it is part of the code. Carrying an answer is not printing a solution sheet: the Player, which has no **Print solution**, still prints the QR code with its answer, and the Generator prints its solution sheet only when asked. Code and QR code are made from one snapshot of the puzzle taken when the dialog opens, at the angle it is shown (not from what has been placed since). **Print** stays disabled, with "Preparing the puzzle code…", until both are ready, never prints by itself afterwards, and does not print old data: a change of the puzzle, its angle or the heading's language makes a new preparation, while changing the options or the game's progress does not.

**Where the QR code leads.** To the Player of the page the app runs from, always: a public page, a fork (which keeps its own path), a page opened from this computer or a private network (localhost, a LAN address, a file). There is no option to change it and no notice about it; a page with no usable address (not http, https or file) falls back to the public Player, `https://tdat00.github.io/pentomino/player/`.

**When the code is too long.** The code is reduced only in an approved order, and each step is announced in the dialog before printing: first the optional solution part is left out of the QR code; then the QR code uses low error correction; and for the rare puzzle whose core still cannot be made into a QR code, the sheet prints no QR code and says "This puzzle is too large for a puzzle code." A code is never cut short, and a failure to prepare it is reported as a failure with **Try again**, never as a puzzle that is too large.

**What has been checked.** The path from the printed first sheet to the same puzzle in the Player (the QR code decoded to the address that **Share** copies, the code opened through the entry dialog, the same board, pieces, clues, angle, name and rating, saved moves restored and Replay) is checked automatically in both apps, in both languages and in the light, dark and high-contrast themes, and at a phone's width. That is not a printed sheet: printing on A4 at 100 % with the 50 mm bar measured, scanning the QR code with real Android and iPhone cameras, typing a printed code on a phone and the clipboard of a real user are checks that the owner does before a release.

## Limits, waiting, and sharing

Boards need **at least 30 playable squares**, with sides ≤**32**. Launch limits are **484 squares on desktop and 360 in phone mode**. Passing browser checks can raise these to **576 and 484**. The Generator page offers no larger boards; the 676-square ceiling is reserved for the command-line tool.

Each generation action targets **one minute** and stops with its results or a clear explanation within **two minutes**. While it runs, its button shows a spinner and "Elapsed: m:ss"; after 30 seconds the button becomes **Cancel**, which keeps finished puzzles.

**A new puzzle is a request, not a guarantee.** For example: “Found 0 of 1 different Hard puzzles within the time limit. The piece set was not changed.” When a set runs out of arrangements the app says so plainly—“Found 6/6 arrangements, the most this piece set allows”—and offers **Generate new piece set**. Some arrangements may differ by only a few pieces. Never silently substitute clue-only variants.

**Play** hands the puzzles to the Player through a portable link that carries the code of every puzzle of the list, `…/player/#p=<code>;<code>;…`, with `&i=<n>` when the puzzle to open is not the first; when the link would be too long (over 16,000 characters, or more than 100 puzzles), only the puzzle to open goes, and the Generator says so. The Player's address can be shared and bookmarked. Neither app imports or exports files. Play opens the whole puzzle list as one pack of at most 512 KiB, so the list is full at that size: New puzzle then says so, and a new puzzle that would not fit is not added.

**QR codes are made on the device.** A bundled encoder draws the symbol from the link text; no puzzle, code or link is sent to a QR service, and the strict content policy is unchanged. The encoder draws the printed first sheet's QR code (see Print on paper); no other screen shows one.

**A puzzle code rebuilds one puzzle exactly.** A seed alone cannot do it, because a puzzle also depends on the piece sets and puzzles made before it. The engine therefore has a puzzle-code layer that turns one puzzle into a short text and back, with no seed, calibration or search. The first part, the **core**, holds the board with its blocked margins and display angle, the whole piece set (its order, each piece's shape as stored, letters and colors), the pre-placed pieces, the name, and the level, score and uniqueness claims; the cores of the 6×10 sample puzzles are 36 to 38 characters. An optional second part after a dot, the **tail**, holds the stored solution. A core alone is a complete, playable puzzle; a tail is only ever an addition, and a damaged tail never changes or hides a good core. Each part ends with four check symbols, so a typing error is noticed; when exactly one wrong symbol would explain it, the layer offers that single correction as a suggestion, and it never applies one or opens a puzzle by itself. Codes use the digits and capital letters without I, L, O and U: a typed O, I or L is read as 0 or 1, and spaces, line breaks and hyphens are ignored. **The Player opens a code from its address or from its entry dialog.** `…/player/#p=<core>` or `…/player/#p=<core>.<tail>` opens one puzzle with no seed; saved progress for the same board, pieces and pre-placed pieces is found again, and a code with a tail opens with its stored answer already known. The address may carry `&i=0` and nothing else about the puzzle: another puzzle number, a repeated key, or `p` together with `pack` or `lib` is refused. **Several puzzles in one address.** The `p` value may hold several codes separated by `;` (at most 100, spaces and a trailing `;` ignored): the Player decodes them one after the other and opens them as one pack in that order, with `&i=<n>` naming the puzzle to open (counted from 0, and it must name one of the codes; with one code it can only be 0). The puzzles must share one board and piece set (the angle of the first is the pack's) and none may repeat; a code that cannot be opened is reported with its number, and no recovery or correction is offered for a list. A one-code address is never rewritten, a list follows the open puzzle with `&i=`. The Generator's **Play** builds this address, and its **Import puzzle** takes such a link and imports all its puzzles. Decoding runs in a background worker, never on the page itself; a browser without module workers says so and opens nothing. A link that cannot be opened leaves the open game, its moves, timer and saved progress exactly as they were. A damaged tail does not open anything by itself: the intact core is kept aside as an offer, and a single-symbol correction is only ever offered, never applied. The Print dialog of both apps makes the code of a puzzle and prints it with a QR code (see Print on paper); no page scans one, and the Player's entry dialog (see Player: solve comfortably) is where a printed code is typed or pasted. The headless generator (`tools\generate.mjs --code`, optional) can print the code of every puzzle it accepts, one per line, exactly as the Print dialog makes it for that puzzle.

**Share sends a puzzle, or the whole list, as a link.** The Player has it: an icon button left of **Print**, on the row of the puzzle name, at its right (the Generator has none; its **Print** carries the same address as a QR code, and **Play** opens the list in the Player). It opens a window with the link of the puzzle that is shown, `…/player/#p=<code>`, in a read-only field, its QR code beside it ("Scan with a phone to play"), and **Copy link**. When the list has more than one puzzle, the switch **Share all puzzles in the list** makes one link for the whole list, `…/player/#p=<code>;<code>…`, opening at the selected puzzle (`&i=<n>`); the window always opens with it off. The code is the very code that the QR code of the printout carries for that puzzle, from the same snapshot (the stored answer and the angle it is shown at) and the same preparation, and the link points at the page the user is on: the Player of the current page's own address (a public page, a fork, or this computer's own `localhost` page alike), never a hardcoded host. Only a page that has no usable address (not http, https or file) falls back to the public Player. While the link is prepared the field says "Preparing the link…" and **Copy link** waits; choosing another puzzle, removing one or turning the view meanwhile prepares the link again, so a link is never shown or copied for the wrong puzzle. "Copied" appears on the button only after the browser has taken the text. When the browser refuses the clipboard, the window stays open with "Could not copy the link. Select and copy it manually.", the link selected in its field, and nothing says "copied". When the link does not fit a QR code, the stored answers are left out first, then the QR code uses low error correction, and only then is there no QR code; each step is noted. A link over 4,000 characters gets a note that some chat apps cut long links; a link over 16,000 characters (or a list of more than 100 puzzles) is not made: "This puzzle is too large to share as a link.", or for the list, a hint to share only the selected puzzle. Escape or **Close** closes the window and returns the focus to **Share**. The texts exist in English and Vietnamese.
